# Resume bullets

Bullets for a CV, true as of the snapshot on 2026-09-23. Each one ends with the claims that back it in the audit at https://jgoetzmann.github.io/numeric-integration-methods-exploration/claims.html, where every claim carries its verdict, limits and evidence.

- Designed and ran an unattended, containerized Runge-Kutta coefficient search (Python, Docker, PowerShell host watchdog); epoch 1 wrote 141,364 scored records over 3,931 cycles and 13 days (2026-08-29 to 2026-09-10). Guarded the scorer: read-only harness, sha256 pin over 14 files, 91 golden and canary cases, 1,908 tests. (claims R11, R12)
- Found a three-stage dyadic method with 2.95x lower held-out error than midpoint, best of eight classical methods, in Q15 fixed point with floor rounding at a 65,536-cycle budget (analytic Cortex-M0+ cost model; 1.01x on traced whole-step cost). In float64 it is 1,992x to 420,975x less accurate than rk4. (claims R1, R5)
- Traced a GCC 13.2.1 build of the integrator step on an instruction-accurate (not cycle-accurate) emulator, reproduced the Python evaluator in 144 of 144 cases, and caught the cost model ranking rk4 and rk38 backwards. Froze epoch 1 rather than rescore it; epoch 2 uses the corrected model. (claims R7, R12)
- Tested the discovered methods on eight out-of-sample problems chosen after the search began; the best had lower Q15 error on 4 of 5 non-stiff ones. Published the misses too: all overflowed on the stiff `robertson_scaled` problem, and a premise test with preset thresholds came back mixed. (claims R4, R9)
- Built a 12-method, 10,000-ODE Runge-Kutta benchmark harness in 2025 (10,279 lines of Python, 11 ODE families) that ranked Dormand-Prince and RK4 lowest in mean error. Audited it in September 2026 and published corrections: evolution was seeded with both under a clipped score, so the rediscovery claim failed. (claims N1, N9, N2)
