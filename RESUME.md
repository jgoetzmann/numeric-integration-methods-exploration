# Resume bullets

Bullets for a CV, true as of the snapshot on 2026-09-23. Each one ends with the claims that back it in the audit at https://jgoetzmann.github.io/numeric-integration-methods-exploration/claims.html, where every claim carries its verdict, limits and evidence.

- Designed and ran an unattended, containerized search for Runge-Kutta coefficients (Python, Docker, a PowerShell host watchdog). Epoch 1 ran from 2026-08-29 to 2026-09-10: 3,931 cycles over 13 days and 141,364 scored records. The scorer stays out of the search's reach: the harness is mounted read-only, a sha256 over 14 pinned files is checked at every start and stored in every record, 91 golden and canary cases must pass before any cycle runs, and the suite collects 1,908 tests. (claims R11, R12)
- Found, through that search, a three-stage dyadic method with 2.95x lower held-out error than midpoint, the best of eight classical methods, inside Q15 fixed point with floor rounding at a 65,536-cycle budget on a modeled Cortex-M0+ (analytic cost model; the lead narrows to 1.01x on a traced whole-step cost basis). Published its float64 boundary with it: in float64 the method is 1,992x to 420,975x less accurate than rk4. (claims R1, R5)
- Caught an error in the project's own cost model: compiled the integrator step with GCC 13.2.1 and traced it under an instruction-accurate (not cycle-accurate) emulator, reproduced the Python evaluator bit for bit in 144 of 144 cases, and found the model had put rk4 and rk38 in the wrong order. Froze epoch 1 and started epoch 2 under the corrected model and a new verifier hash instead of rescoring the old archive. (claims R7, R12)
- Checked the result out of sample and published the negative results with the positive ones: on eight problems chosen after the search began, the best discovered method had lower Q15 error on 4 of 5 non-stiff problems, and every discovered method overflowed on the stiff `robertson_scaled` problem. The project's premise test, with thresholds set before the search, came back mixed. (claims R4, R9)
- Built a 12-method, 10,000-ODE benchmark harness for Runge-Kutta tables in 2025: a generic Butcher-table stepper, an 11-family ODE generator with stiff cases, a SciPy reference solver and parallel scoring, in 10,279 lines of Python. Audited its conclusions in September 2026 and published corrections: the benchmark ranked Dormand-Prince and RK4 lowest in mean error, but the evolutionary search had been seeded with both tables under a clipped score, so the original claim that it rediscovered them did not hold. (claims N1, N9, N2)
